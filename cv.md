## Snapshot — September 2026

Independent researcher based in South Korea. All listed conference works are sole-authored unless otherwise noted.

In my first academic submission season beginning in January 2026, my work has been accepted across multiple international venues in STS, platform governance, internet research, labour studies, digital governance, industrial relations, political science, media accountability, critical theory, and public administration / collaborative governance.

The work is primarily theoretical, diagnostic, comparative, and framework-oriented. It develops conceptual and empirical tools for identifying recurring structural patterns across AI governance, platform systems, labour pressure, working time, labour-market entry, digitally mediated political judgment, institutional responsibility, and repair.

By September 2026, I had completed or converted presentations at the ILERA Asian Regional Congress and the Midlands Conference in Critical Thought. Additional accepted work includes 4S 2026 and IIAS 2026. Several other accepted contributions — AoIR 2026, EASST 2026, EGOV-CeDEM-ePart / IFIP EGOV 2026, BSA Work, Employment & Society 2026, HCII / AI-HCI 2026, and the IPSA Regional Conference in Wrocław 2026 — were not converted because registration, travel, accommodation, proceedings, or participation funding constraints prevented completion.

Six working papers are publicly available on SSRN.

## Current position

Independent researcher and analytical writer working on labour-market institutions, AI, governance, platform systems, political communication, household economic security, and institutional responsibility.

Current focus areas include:

- working time, demographic ageing, labour-income dependence, and productivity sharing
- labour-market entry, first credibility, recruitment, and public employment services
- household income sufficiency and economic security
- AI-era selection, visibility, responsibility, and repair
- platform governance and access infrastructures
- digital governance, legislation, and responsibility recoverability
- digitally mediated political judgment, media visibility, and democratic resilience
- performance attribution, burden transfer, and institutional bottlenecks
- framework-based diagnostic and comparative research
