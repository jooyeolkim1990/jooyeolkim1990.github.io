# AI, Mathematics, and the Control of Discovery
## Retrospective development record

**Committed publicly:** 2026-11-24

This directory is a retrospective record reconstructed from a private ChatGPT conversation that developed across September–November 2026.

The dated files below **do not claim contemporaneous public publication**. Their dates indicate when the corresponding line of thought had emerged in the conversation. The Git history begins on 2026-11-24.

The purpose of this record is personal and intellectual: to preserve how the argument changed as the public controversy itself changed.

## Development sequence

1. **2026-09-12 — Human first discovery**
   - The initial distinction between mathematics itself and the scarcity of human first discovery.
   - Search-cost compression, alchemy-to-chemistry, Go after AlphaGo, and geographic exploration.

2. **2026-09-13 — Treasure hunt and community norms**
   - A more sympathetic account of why mathematicians could reasonably resent AI firms entering a research environment they had built.
   - The distinction between respecting a research community and reserving an open problem for human solvers.

3. **2026-09-19 — The implicit addressee of an open problem**
   - "Humanity does not yet know" was separated from "a human should know first."
   - The argument was extended from named open problems to mathematical territory that humans had not yet formulated as problems.
   - Core line: **You cannot reserve for yourself what you do not yet know exists.**

4. **2026-10-31 — Authority and control**
   - AGMAI's recommendations about company-designated mathematicians shifted the issue from discovery alone to the allocation of authority.
   - The symmetry question emerged: if corporate selection of mathematicians is an exercise of power, why should academic allocation of prestige be treated as politically neutral?
   - The "remote control" metaphor became the organizing frame.

5. **2026-11-24 — Retrospective after the Riemann-hypothesis episode**
   - New AI-mathematics developments made the earlier distinction between formal solution, human understanding, and institutional control more concrete.
   - This file set was created as a retrospective record.

6. **2026-11-24 — Status, expertise, and the symmetry test**
   - Expertise was separated from symbolic authority.
   - A status-symmetry test was formulated: hold the AI contribution, proof, understanding, verification, and release process constant, then ask whether the community's standards change when the mathematician's status changes.
   - Discovery rights were distinguished more sharply from validation and interpretation rights.

## Current core position

The mathematical community has legitimate standing to demand respect for the research norms it has built: attribution, verification, priority, responsible release, and room for independent interpretation.

That standing does not automatically create a human priority right over undiscovered mathematical truth.

AI companies do not own mathematics.

The existing mathematical establishment does not own mathematics either.

The dispute increasingly concerns who controls discovery, verification, interpretation, visibility, and the allocation of mathematical authority.
